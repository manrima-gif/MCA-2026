Music data is hard to access, curate, and distribute because notation, audio, metadata, and context are held by different institutions under different rules. Trying to find all types of data for each music shows how easily a tune can be heard but not properly documented. The Balkan folk theme sharpens this: melodies were passed on orally, and early recordings survive mainly on fragile 78 rpm discs. Data is gathered by ethnomusicologists, archives, record labels, and today by YouTube uploaders and MuseScore transcribers. MuseScore and IMSLP present notation well and are searchable, but arrangements often have unclear rights and rarely name their source recording. YouTube offers quick access to sound but seldom gives the original label or date. DAHR and the Internet Archive hold richer historical metadata, but it is inconsistent and harder to search. Analysis is uneven because notation, sound, and context sit in unlinked sources. The main strength of current approaches is breadth; the main weakness is fragmentation.\]
